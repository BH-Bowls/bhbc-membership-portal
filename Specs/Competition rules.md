# **BHBC Club Competitions — Rules & Markers' Instructions**

## **General Rules**

1. Within 7 days of a round commencing, the challenger (first name on the draw) should offer the opponent a minimum of three reasonable dates, at least one of which must be a weekend. This does not apply to Drawn Triples.  
2. Competitions may not be played on Club Nights.  
3. Dress code: grey or navy trousers/skirts and white or club tops must be worn for all club competitions.

See **Marker Responsibilities** below for who arranges markers in singles competitions.

## **Standard Scoring Procedure**

This procedure applies to all competitions except where a competition's own instructions say otherwise (Two Wood, Veterans, Australian Pairs and Centenary each have variations noted under their own heading).

1. Before play begins, two trial ends are played. Toss a coin — the challenger calls. Whoever wins the toss chooses whether to take the mat or give it away for the first trial end. The mat passes to the other player for the second trial end, then reverts back to whichever player held it for the first trial end, who takes the mat for the first end of the match proper.  
2. Each player/pair/team plays their full complement of woods before the end is scored.  
3. The score is decided by counting the woods of the winning side that are closer to the jack than the opponent's closest wood.  
4. Record the points scored by the winning side on the scorecard; the losing side scores 0 for that end.  
5. Keep a running total for both sides after each end.  
6. In the event of a tied jack or a dead end, replay the end with the same player retaining the mat. The replayed end still counts as one end towards the ends total.  
7. For competitions played to a **fixed number of ends**, if the scores are level once all ends have been played, toss a coin to decide who has the mat and play one extra end to determine the winner.  
8. At the conclusion of play, the players sign the scorecard and place it in the clubhouse "Completed Competitions Box".

   ## **Marker Responsibilities**

A marker is required for all singles competitions. It is the responsibility of the challenger to organise a marker; a list of markers is available on the portal. The tournament committee organises markers for the semi-finals and finals.

A marker is not required for pairs and triples competitions. Each team maintains its own scorecard and should check it with the scoreboard at the end of each end.

**Markers' Instructions:** Standard Scoring Procedure applies. Play continues until one player reaches 21 points.

## **Men's and Ladies Two Wood**

**Format:** Singles

**Description:** Play to 21 ends, 2 woods each. Only woods within one yard of the jack score.

**Markers' Instructions:** Standard Scoring Procedure applies, with the following variations:

1. Each player plays 2 woods per end (not 4).  
2. Before scoring, check which woods (if any) are within one yard of the jack — use a measuring aid if in doubt. Any wood outside one yard does not count, even if it is the closest wood on the green.  
3. Score using only woods within the one-yard zone: the winning player scores one point for each qualifying wood closer to the jack than the opponent's closest qualifying wood.  
4. If neither player has a wood within one yard of the jack, the end is scored as 0–0 (a "blank" end).  
5. Play is **to 21 ends**, not 21 points — the match finishes after 21 ends have been played, or earlier if one player has an unassailable lead that the remaining ends cannot overturn. The player with the higher points total at that point wins.

   ## **Handicap**

**Format:** Singles

**Description:** Play to 21 points, 4 woods each. All members start with a handicap of 0; a player's handicap increases as they win competitions, so a higher handicap reflects a stronger competitive record. The difference between the two players' handicaps is set on the scoreboard at the start of play — the player with the lower handicap receives the head start. For example, if player 1 has a handicap of 6 and player 2 has a handicap of 4, player 2 starts with 2 points already showing on the scorecard and scoreboard.

**Markers' Instructions:** Standard Scoring Procedure applies, with the following addition:

1. Before play begins, check the draw sheet for each player's handicap and set the scoreboard accordingly, giving the head start to the player with the lower handicap.  
2. Double-check the scoreboard reflects the handicap correctly before the first end is played — this is the most common source of error in this competition.  
3. Play continues until one player reaches 21 points.

   ## **Veterans**

**Format:** Singles

**Description:** Play to 100 points, 4 woods each. Special scoring system — see marker's instructions.

**Markers' Instructions:**

Veterans does **not** use the Standard Scoring Procedure. Points are awarded to whichever player owns each of the **three woods nearest the jack**, regardless of which player played them. Use the special Veterans scorecard, which has a 5/3/1 box for each player on every row.

1. Each player plays all 4 woods per end as normal.  
2. Once all woods are played, identify the three woods nearest the jack (irrespective of owner):  
   * Nearest wood: **5 points**  
   * Second nearest wood: **3 points**  
   * Third nearest wood: **1 point**  
   * This gives a total of **9 points to be shared** between the two players every end.  
3. Tick the box for the player who owns the nearest wood (5), the player who owns the second nearest wood (3), and the player who owns the third nearest wood (1). Note that one player could own two or even all three of the scoring woods in an end.  
4. Add each player's points for the end to their running total.  
5. **Check your totals:** the scorecard has the expected combined grand total pre-printed in the centre of each row (9 after end 1, 18 after end 2, 27 after end 3 … 81 after end 9, and so on, increasing by 9 each end). After updating both players' running totals, confirm they add up to the pre-printed figure for that end. If they don't, recheck your scoring for that end before play continues.  
6. Play continues until one player reaches 100 points.

   ## **Married Pairs and Drawn Pairs**

**Format:** Pairs

**Description:** Play to 18 ends, 4 woods each.

Standard Scoring Procedure applies. Each player plays 4 woods (8 woods per end per pair). Play is to **18 ends** — the match finishes after 18 ends, and the pair with the higher total wins.

## **Australian Pairs**

**Format:** Pairs

**Description:** Play to 18 ends, 4 woods each. Players alternate positions each end, with woods played in a 2, then 4, then 2 sequence.

Standard Scoring Procedure applies for scoring the end once all woods are played, with the following variations:

1. Players alternate who leads and who plays second each end.  
2. Woods are played in a **2–4–2 sequence** rather than each player playing all their woods consecutively:  
   * The two leads play 2 woods each first.  
   * The two seconds then play 4 woods each.  
   * The two leads finish by playing their remaining 2 woods each.  
3. Play is to **18 ends**.  
   ---

   ## **Drawn Triples**

**Format:** Triples

**Description:** Play to 18 ends, 3 woods each.

Standard Scoring Procedure applies. Each of the three players per team plays 3 woods per end (9 woods per end per team). Play is to **18 ends**.

## **Centenary**

**Format:** Singles

**Description:** Best of three sets, each set comprising seven ends (extra end if tied). 4 woods each. Played on a special scorecard.

**Markers' Instructions:** Standard Scoring Procedure applies for scoring each end, with the following variations:

1. Use the special Centenary scorecard, which has a clear break marked between sets.  
2. A set consists of **seven ends**. Add up each player's points across the seven ends to determine the set winner (most points wins the set).  
3. If the two players are tied on points at the end of the seventh end, toss a coin and play an **extra end** to decide the set.  
4. If a set is won early — i.e. one player has an unassailable lead before all seven ends have been played — stop the set at that point; there is no need to play out the remaining ends.  
5. Mark the set result clearly at the break on the scorecard before starting the next set.  
6. The match is decided on a **best-of-three-sets** basis — first player to win two sets wins the match.

**Mat allocation between sets:**

* **Set 1:** toss a coin as per the Standard Scoring Procedure to decide who takes the mat first.  
* **Set 2:** the mat goes to whichever player did **not** have the mat for the first end of Set 1 (i.e. it swaps automatically — no toss).  
* **Set 3 (if needed):** toss a coin again to decide the mat, as this set is a tie-break.  
* If a set finishes early (e.g. won 10–4 after 6 ends, with the result already decided), the mat allocation for the next set is still based on who *would* have had the mat for the next end had the set continued — i.e. work out who would have had the mat on the end after the set ended, and give that player the mat for the following set.
